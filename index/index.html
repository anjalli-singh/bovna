Sleep Better, Starting Tonight

Good sleep is built from small daily habits. Try these simple changes for two weeks and notice the difference.

Start now

What you need to know

1

Fixed schedule

Wake up at the same time every day, even on weekends. A steady body clock makes falling asleep easier.

2

Dark and cool

Keep the room dark, quiet and cool, roughly 16 to 19°C, with a comfortable mattress and pillow.

3

Wind down

Spend the last 30 to 60 minutes before bed away from bright screens and stressful work.

🌙

Your habits shape your nights

Most adults need around seven to nine hours of sleep. Light, caffeine and irregular timing quietly push that sleep away.

You do not need a perfect routine, just a repeatable one that tells your body it is time to rest.

A routine for tonight

1

Fix your wake time

Choose a time you can keep every day and set one alarm.

2

Cut caffeine early

Avoid tea, coffee and energy drinks for at least six hours before bed.

3

Dim the lights

Lower the lights and put screens away 30 to 60 minutes before sleep.

4

Do not toss and turn

If you are awake for about 20 minutes, get up, do something calm, and return when sleepy.

Common questions

Is sleeping in on weekends bad?

A small shift is fine, but big changes in wake time confuse your body clock and make Monday harder.

Do naps hurt night sleep?

Short naps of 20 to 30 minutes, taken before mid-afternoon, usually do not.

When should I see a doctor?

If you have long-term trouble sleeping, loud snoring with pauses in breathing, or heavy daytime sleepiness. This guide is general information, not medical advice.
